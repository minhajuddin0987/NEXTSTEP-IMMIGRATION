Belgium Student Visa — Step by Step

Everything you need to know to get your university offer letter and then submit your visa application at the Belgian Embassy in Dhaka.

🎓
Step 01 — University Application
Getting Your Offer Letter
  • ✓
    Academic Transcripts — SSC, HSC, Bachelor's certificates with certified English translations if originals are in Bengali
  • ✓
    Statement of Purpose (SOP) — A compelling 600–1,000 word essay explaining your study goals and why Belgium
  • ✓
    CV / Europass Resume — Academic and professional background; Europass format preferred by Belgian universities
  • ✓
    English Proficiency — IELTS minimum 6.0–6.5, or TOEFL, or proof of English-medium education at previous institution
  • ✓
    Letters of Recommendation — 2 academic or professional references written on official letterhead
  • ✓
    Valid Passport — At least 18 months remaining validity from your intended start date
  • ✓
    Application Form & Fee — Submitted through each university's online admissions portal; fees vary per institution
  • ✓
    Portfolio / Work Samples — Required only for arts, design, or architecture programs
🏛️
Step 02 — Embassy Submission
Visa Application Documents
  • ✓
    University Acceptance Letter — Official offer letter from the Belgian university confirming your enrollment
  • ✓
    Valid Passport — Original + 2 clear photocopies; minimum 12 months validity beyond your planned study duration
  • ✓
    Type D Long-Stay Visa Form — Completed, signed and dated student visa application form
  • ✓
    Biometric Passport Photos — 2 recent photos meeting Belgian visa standards (white background, 35×45mm)
  • ✓
    Financial Proof — Minimum €650/month required; 6 months bank statements, sponsorship letter, or scholarship confirmation
  • ✓
    Health Insurance — Comprehensive coverage valid for Belgium and the full Schengen area for minimum 12 months
  • ✓
    Accommodation Proof — Confirmed university dormitory booking or signed rental agreement in Belgium
  • ✓
    Notarized Academic Certificates — All educational certificates (SSC, HSC, Bachelor's) officially notarized
  • ✓
    Clean Criminal Record — From Bangladesh Police, attested and apostilled by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs
  • ✓
    Medical Certificate — Recent health checkup certificate from a certified physician
  • ✓
    Visa Fee — Approximately €180 (non-refundable), paid at time of submission

Living in Belgium — The Complete Student Guide

Everything an international student from Bangladesh needs to know before and after arriving in Belgium — from renting a room to finding a part-time job and planning your future in Europe.

💰 Monthly Budget
Cost of Living
€
Total estimate: €850 – €1,200/month (excluding tuition)
🏠
Rent (shared room/kot): €350 – €600/month
🛒
Groceries & food: €200 – €300/month; supermarkets like Lidl/Aldi are student-friendly
🚌
Transport + phone: €30 – €60/month with student discounts
🏥
Health insurance: ~€10–15/month (mandatory mutualité)
💡 Brussels is pricier than Ghent or Leuven. Budget €100–200 more/month in the capital.
🏠 Where to Live
Student Accommodation
🏢
University "Kot" (dorm): €350–500/month usually including utilities — apply as early as possible
🏡
Private shared room: €400–650 in Brussels/Leuven; cheaper in Ghent/Liège
🔑
Deposit required: Typically 2–3 months rent upfront
🌐
Find rooms on: Kotweb.be, Immoweb.be, Facebook groups "Rooms in Belgium"
📅
Start searching 2–3 months before your arrival date — spots go fast
💡 Ask your university's international office first — they often have reserved student housing lists.
🩺 Health & Insurance
Healthcare in Belgium
✅
Mandatory: Register with a Belgian mutualité (health insurance fund) within 3 months of arrival
🏛️
Options: Mutualité Chrétienne (MC), Partenamut, Mutualité Neutre — all offer student rates
💊
Coverage: Reimburses 70–80% of doctor visits, prescriptions, and specialist fees
💶
Student cost: Approximately €10–15/month after student reduction applied
🏥
Most universities have an on-campus student health service for free basic consultations
💡 Keep your mutualité card with you at all times. Third-party payment means you rarely pay upfront at a GP.
💼 Work Rights
Part-Time Jobs & Work Rules
⏱
20 hours/week maximum during academic year; full-time during July–August holidays
💶
Minimum wage: ~€11.50/hour gross; popular jobs: hospitality, retail, campus assistant, delivery
📄
Work permit: Non-EU students need a permit — your employer applies for it alongside your student residence card
🎯
Students can earn up to €6,000 tax-free per year under the student worker scheme
📱
Find jobs via StudentJob.be, Jobat.be and university job boards
💡 Working legally protects you and builds a Belgian work history — important for future visa renewals.
🚆 Getting Around
Transport & Student Perks
🚍
Brussels (STIB): Annual student pass ~€25/year when purchased through university — covers all metro, tram, bus
🚂
Trains (SNCB): "Go Pass 1" at €7.60 per single journey; student monthly passes heavily discounted
🚴
Cycling: Belgium is very bike-friendly — Villo! (Brussels), Blue-bike (nationwide) are affordable bike-sharing
🌍
Schengen travel: Day trips to Paris (1.5h), Amsterdam (2h), and Cologne (2h) by train
🎭
Student ID discounts: 50% off museums, theaters, cinema, and cultural events across Belgium
💡 Most Belgian students cycle everywhere — budget €150–250 for a second-hand bike from Vinted or local markets.
🚀 After Graduation
Post-Study & Career Paths
📆
Orientation Year Permit: 12-month residence permit after graduation to search for employment — no job needed upfront
💼
Once employed, switch to a Work Permit B (Single Permit) — pathway to permanent residency after 5 years
🏛️
EU Institutions in Brussels: European Commission, Parliament, NATO — all hire international graduates
📈
High demand for STEM, business, logistics, IT, and finance professionals — Belgium is a major European hub
🌱
After 5 years of legal residence you can apply for long-term EC residence permit
💡 NEXTSTEP can guide you on orientation year applications — contact us after graduation too.
🏦 Admin & Banking
Banking & Registration
🏦
Open a bank account: BNP Paribas Fortis, KBC, ING, Belfius — all offer free student accounts. Bring passport, residence certificate, and enrollment letter
📍
Commune registration: Register at your local commune (city hall) within 8 days of arrival — required by Belgian law
🪪
After commune registration, you receive a foreigner's ID card (Annex 15/35) — this is your official residence document
📶
SIM card: Base, Proximus, Orange Belgium — student SIMs from €10–20/month including data
💡 Commune registration is mandatory — not optional. Do it within your first week.
🗣️ Language & Culture
Language & Integration
🇬🇧
English: Widely spoken, especially in Brussels and at all major universities — you will manage easily
🇳🇱
Dutch (Flemish): Spoken in Ghent, Leuven, Antwerp — learning basics helps greatly with daily life
🇫🇷
French: Spoken in Brussels and Wallonia — useful for social life and many jobs in the capital
📚
Free language courses: Available through universities, NT2 centers, and public libraries — take advantage of these
🌍
Belgium is extremely multicultural — over 35% of Brussels residents are non-Belgian. You will feel at home.
💡 Even basic Dutch or French dramatically improves your job prospects and social integration in Belgium.
📌
Important reminder: Commune registration must be done within 8 days of arrival. Health insurance (mutualité) must be set up within 3 months. Both are legally required. NEXTSTEP Immigration provides full post-arrival guidance to help you complete these steps smoothly upon landing in Belgium.
