Italy Student Visa — Step by Step

Everything you need to get your Italian university offer letter and then submit your student visa at the Italian Embassy in Dhaka.

🎓
Step 01 — University Application
Getting Your Offer Letter
  • ✓
    Academic Transcripts & Certificates — SSC, HSC, Bachelor's with certified English or Italian translations attached
  • ✓
    Dichiarazione di Valore (DoV) — Official declaration of value of your Bangladeshi degree, issued by the Italian Embassy in Dhaka — required for academic recognition in Italy
  • ✓
    Motivational / Cover Letter — 500–800 words explaining your reasons for choosing this specific program and Italy
  • ✓
    Language Certificate — IELTS minimum 5.5–6.0 for English-taught programs; B2 Italian (CILS/PLIDA) for Italian-taught programs
  • ✓
    CV / Europass Resume — Detailing your academic background, work experience, and any extracurricular activities
  • ✓
    Letters of Recommendation — 2 strong references from professors or employers on official institutional letterhead
  • ✓
    Valid Passport — Clear copy of personal information pages; at least 18 months validity remaining
  • ✓
    University Entrance Test (if required) — Medicine and architecture programs require IMAT or TOLC test — check your university's requirements carefully
  • ✓
    Universitaly Pre-Enrollment — Register and pre-enroll at universitaly.it (the official Italian government student portal) before applying to many universities
🏛️
Step 02 — Embassy Submission
Visa Application Documents
  • ✓
    Type D National Long-Stay Visa Form — Completed, signed and dated student visa application; available at Italian Embassy Dhaka
  • ✓
    University Acceptance / Enrollment Letter — Official admission confirmation from your Italian university stating program name, duration, and start date
  • ✓
    Universitaly Pre-Enrollment Receipt — Printed confirmation from the Italian government portal (where applicable to your university)
  • ✓
    Valid Passport — Original + 2 clear photocopies; at least 6 months validity beyond the intended visa period
  • ✓
    Biometric Passport Photos — 2 recent passport-sized photographs on white background
  • ✓
    Financial Proof — Minimum ~€6,000–6,500/year; 6 months bank statements, or scholarship letter, or sponsor's declaration and documents
  • ✓
    Accommodation Proof — University dormitory booking confirmation, signed rental agreement, or host declaration in Italy
  • ✓
    Dichiarazione di Valore (DoV) — Issued by the Italian Embassy in Bangladesh — absolutely essential for recognition of your Bangladeshi degree
  • ✓
    Health Insurance — Valid comprehensive health coverage for Italy, minimum 12 months duration, covering emergency and hospitalization
  • ✓
    Clean Criminal Record — Issued by Bangladesh Police, officially attested by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs
  • ✓
    Medical Certificate — Recent certificate from a certified physician confirming you are in good health
  • ✓
    Visa Fee — Approximately €50 (non-refundable Italian student visa fee), paid at the Embassy on day of submission

Living in Italy — The Complete Student Guide

Everything a Bangladeshi student needs to know before and after arriving in Italy — from getting your Codice Fiscale to finding accommodation, working part-time, and building your future in Europe.

💶 Monthly Budget
Cost of Living in Italy
€
Total estimate: €700–€1,000/month (€1,200–1,500 in Milan or Rome)
🏠
Rent (shared room): €300–€550/month; cheaper in Turin, Padua, Naples
🛒
Groceries: €150–200/month; local markets (mercato) are excellent and cheap
🚌
Transport pass (student): €20–35/month for city buses, metro and trams
🍕
University canteen (mensa): Full meals for only €4–7 — heavily subsidized for students
💡 DSU regional grants can cover tuition + provide monthly stipends (€150–300) for low-income international students — apply early!
🪪 First Priority
Codice Fiscale — Your Essential ID
⭐
What it is: Italy's tax code — a unique personal identifier absolutely required for everything in Italy
📋
You need it for: Opening a bank account, renting a flat, getting a SIM card, registering at university, healthcare
🏛️
How to get it: Visit the Italian Embassy in Dhaka before departure, or the Agenzia delle Entrate office in Italy upon arrival
💰
Cost: Completely free — takes 15–30 minutes to issue
📅
Get it before you leave Bangladesh if possible — saves time and stress upon arrival
💡 NEXTSTEP can guide you in obtaining your Codice Fiscale from the Italian Embassy in Dhaka before departure.
🩺 Health & Insurance
Healthcare — SSN & Insurance
🏥
SSN (National Health Service): Voluntary registration for international students costs ~€150/year — gives access to full Italian public healthcare
👨‍⚕️
With SSN: choose a medico di base (GP) for free; access to hospitals, specialists, and subsidized medicine
🔒
Private insurance: Required for visa (€120–300/year); but SSN is recommended for your long-term stay
💊
Prescriptions: €2–4 co-payment with SSN; private costs €20–40 per medicine without it
🏫
Most universities have a Centro Medico Studentesco (student health center) for free basic consultations
💡 Register with SSN within your first month in Italy — it is affordable and provides real protection throughout your studies.
📄 Residence Permit
Permesso di Soggiorno
⚡
Apply within 8 days of arrival — mandatory for all non-EU students staying more than 90 days
📮
How to apply: Get the "kit" from any Post Office (Sportello Amico) — fee is approximately €70–100
⏳
Processing time: 3–6 months; you receive a ricevuta (receipt) immediately which serves as temporary proof of legal stay
🔄
Must be renewed each academic year — bring your enrollment confirmation for each renewal
📌
Required documents: passport, visa, enrollment letter, financial proof, accommodation proof, Codice Fiscale, photos
💡 Never miss the 8-day deadline — overstaying without applying is a serious legal issue in Italy.
💼 Work Rights
Working Part-Time in Italy
⏱
20 hours/week allowed with a student residence permit; full-time during official academic breaks
💶
Typical pay: €8–12/hour in restaurants, retail, tutoring, and university language assistant roles
🇮🇹
Italian language is key: Even basic Italian (A2–B1) dramatically improves your job opportunities in daily life sectors
🎓
After 1st year: eligible for tirocini (internships) — many companies in fashion, design, and tech hire university interns
🌍
Find jobs on Indeed.it, InfoJobs.it and through your university's career center
💡 Tourism sector (summer) offers full-time hours during July–September — great income during academic break.
🚀 After Graduation
Post-Study Opportunities
📆
Job-search permit: After degree, apply for a 12-month "Permesso di Soggiorno per Ricerca Lavoro" to stay and find employment
💼
Once employed, convert to a work permit — after 5 years of legal residence you can apply for EC long-term permit
🏭
Strong demand for graduates in engineering, IT, fashion & design, medicine, and finance across Italy's major cities
🌱
Italy Startup Visa: Innovative entrepreneurs can apply for a special startup permit to launch a business in Italy
🇪🇺
An Italian degree is EU-recognized — opens doors across all 27 EU countries, not just Italy
💡 NEXTSTEP provides guidance for post-graduation residence permit applications — we support you beyond just the visa.
🏠 Accommodation
Finding Housing in Italy
🏫
University residences (Residenze): €250–400/month — very limited spots, apply immediately after acceptance
🏠
Private shared rooms: €350–600 in Milan/Rome; €250–400 in Bologna, Turin, and Padua
🎓
DSU housing: If you qualify for the regional scholarship (ISEE), you may get heavily subsidized or free housing
🌐
Find rooms on: Idealista.it, Subito.it, Uniplaces, Spotahome, and Facebook groups like "Affitti Bologna Studenti"
⚠️
Contracts usually require 3 months deposit — budget for this before departure
💡 Start your housing search 2–3 months before arrival. Italian landlords often prefer someone who can view in person — arrive before semester if possible.
🚌 Getting Around
Transport & Student Perks
🚍
City transport: Monthly student passes €20–35; covers bus, metro, and tram in most cities
🚂
National trains: Trenitalia and Italo offer student discounts up to 30%; "Carta Verde" youth rail card available
🏛️
Museums FREE / discounted: Colosseum, Uffizi, Vatican Museums — free or €2 for under-25 or students
🎫
Carta dello Studente: National student discount card — 30% off trains, discounts on cinema, theatre, and shops
🌍
Travel: Schengen zone — weekend trips to France, Spain, Greece, and Croatia with no border checks
💡 In smaller Italian cities like Bologna and Padua, cycling is extremely popular — a cheap second-hand bike is a great investment.
🗣️ Language & Culture
Italian Language & Integration
🇮🇹
Italian is essential for daily life — supermarkets, doctors, landlords, and most locals speak little English outside tourist areas
🇬🇧
English-taught programs available widely at Master's level — check your university's international catalog before applying
📚
Free Italian courses: Available at CPIA (public adult education centers), university language centers, and online via Duolingo and RAI
🎓
University tandem programs: Practice Italian with native students in exchange for English — great for friendship and language
🌍
Italian culture values warmth, food, and social connection — joining local student clubs integrates you fast
💡 Start learning Italian before you leave Bangladesh — even A1 level makes a huge difference to your first weeks of life in Italy.
📌
Critical reminders for Italy: Apply for your Permesso di Soggiorno within 8 days of arrival. Get your Codice Fiscale as early as possible — from the Italian Embassy in Dhaka before you depart. The Dichiarazione di Valore (DoV) is essential for your degree recognition — NEXTSTEP Immigration assists you with this process. Do not leave Italy without renewing your residence permit before it expires.
